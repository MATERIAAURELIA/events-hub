✦ SCIENCE ✦
The Iron Bar and the Feather
There's a law in psychophysics called Weber-Fechner: we sense change relative to what's already there. The more noise in the system, the less we can feel.

Movement teacher Ido Portal explains it with an image I love. Blindfolded, hold an iron bar and a fly lands on it: you feel nothing. Hold a feather, and you feel the fly. "We are the iron bar or the feather."

Tension and sensitivity are linked: the tighter we hold, the less we can sense. Hold your arm tight and you can barely feel it. Soften it, and even a whisper of sensation comes through.

I've thought a lot about how to give our guests the best experience, and what practices can help us settle, so we can actually receive as we rest.

You'll see me keep advocating for shaking, moving, emptying out, dancing and stretching to clear the noise, so that when the sound comes, you can actually receive it. It's the whole idea behind our upcoming Music Movement Medicine offerings.

✦ WHAT IS FASCIA? ✦
Less Friction, More Signal
Fascia is the web of connective tissue that wraps and links everything in us: muscle, organ, bone. It's also a communication pathway. When it's stressed and bound up, the signals get interrupted, and the noise rises.

That's Weber-Fechner again, living in our tissue. Fascia maneuvers reduce the friction, so the signal can come through, and we can feel more.
